**Deep convolutional networks** stack many convolutional layers so that each one builds on the patterns found by the last. Their history runs through three landmark designs: **LeNet-5** (1998) read handwritten digits, **AlexNet** (2012) started the deep learning boom on photographs, and **ResNet** (2015) showed how to train networks with more than a hundred layers.<Cite id="d2l-modern-cnn" /> The ideas they introduced, especially residual connections, are now used far beyond images, including inside every Transformer.

## Three landmark networks

| Network | Year | Depth | Parameters | Result | What it changed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LeNet-5 | 1998 | 7 layers | about 60,000 | 0.95% test error on MNIST | Convolution, subsampling and backpropagation on real documents |
| AlexNet | 2012 | 8 learned layers | about 60 million | 15.3% top-5 error on ImageNet (runner-up 26.2%) | ReLU, dropout, data augmentation, GPU training |
| ResNet | 2015 | up to 152 layers | 11-60 million | 3.57% top-5 error (ensemble) | Residual (skip) connections |

**LeNet-5**, by LeCun, Bottou, Bengio and Haffner, took 32×32 images through two convolution-and-subsampling stages and three fully connected layers.<Cite id="lecun1998" /> The same paper introduced the <Term slug="mnist" /> benchmark and described a deployed system for reading bank cheques.<Cite id="lecun1998" /> The digits project's CNN is a close modern descendant.

**AlexNet**, by Krizhevsky, Sutskever and Hinton, won the 2012 ImageNet challenge by a wide margin with five convolutional and three fully connected layers trained on two GPUs.<Cite id="krizhevsky2012" /> Its ingredients are still standard: <Term slug="relu" /> activations, which trained several times faster than tanh, <Term slug="dropout" /> in the fully connected layers, and <Term slug="data-augmentation" /> with crops and flips.<Cite id="krizhevsky2012" /> CNNs were not new; AlexNet's contribution was scale and training technique.<Cite id="d2l-modern-cnn" />

## The degradation problem

After AlexNet, deeper usually meant better, but only up to a point. He, Zhang, Ren and Sun observed that a plain 56-layer network had a higher **training** error than a 20-layer one on CIFAR-10.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" /> This is not <Term slug="overfitting" />, which would lower training error. It is an optimisation failure: the deeper network could copy the shallow one and set its extra layers to the identity, yet gradient descent did not find such a solution.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" />

The obvious suspect is the <Term slug="vanishing-gradient" /> problem: the gradient reaching an early layer is a product of one factor per layer, and twenty factors of 0.5 multiply to about one millionth. He and colleagues ruled it out for their networks, which used batch normalization and had healthy gradients, and concluded that stacks of non-linear layers simply find it hard to fit an identity-like mapping.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" /> Residual connections address both difficulties at once.<Cite id="d2l-modern-cnn" />

## Residual connections

ResNet's fix is a <Term slug="residual-connection" />: each block computes a correction $F(x)$ with a couple of convolutions and adds its own input back, $y = F(x) + x$.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" />

<Analogy breaks="The corrections are not small in general; the framing describes the structure, not a limit on how much a block may change.">
A residual block edits a draft instead of rewriting it from scratch. It receives the current draft and only writes corrections, and if it has nothing useful to add it leaves the draft alone.
</Analogy>

He and colleagues later made the direct-path argument precise: with pure identity shortcuts, the signal and its gradient can travel between any two blocks without being multiplied by the layers in between.<Cite id="he2016-identity" /> When a block changes the number of channels or the resolution, the shortcut uses a 1×1 convolution so the shapes match.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" /> With these blocks, a 152-layer network won the 2015 ImageNet challenge.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" />

A minimal residual block in PyTorch looks like this:

```python
class ResidualBlock(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, channels):
        super().__init__()
        self.conv1 = nn.Conv2d(channels, channels, 3, padding=1, bias=False)
        self.bn1 = nn.BatchNorm2d(channels)
        self.conv2 = nn.Conv2d(channels, channels, 3, padding=1, bias=False)
        self.bn2 = nn.BatchNorm2d(channels)

    def forward(self, x):
        out = torch.relu(self.bn1(self.conv1(x)))
        out = self.bn2(self.conv2(out))
        return torch.relu(out + x)      # the skip connection
```

## Batch normalization in CNNs

<Term slug="batch-normalization" />, introduced by Ioffe and Szegedy, standardises each channel to mean 0 and variance 1 over the mini-batch, then applies a learned scale $\gamma$ and shift $\beta$.<Cite id="ioffe2015" /> It allowed higher learning rates and faster training, and ResNet places it after every convolution.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" /> In a CNN, `nn.BatchNorm2d(C)` computes the statistics over the batch and all spatial positions, so it learns just two numbers per channel.<Cite id="pt-batchnorm2d" />

Two practical rules follow. A convolution directly before BatchNorm does not need its own bias, because the normalisation subtracts it again (hence `bias=False` above). And BatchNorm behaves differently in training and evaluation: `model.eval()` makes it use running averages collected during training instead of the current batch's statistics.<Cite id="pt-batchnorm2d" />

## Global average pooling

In the digits CNN, 95% of the 421,642 parameters sit in the first fully connected layer. Lin, Chen and Yan proposed **global average pooling** in *Network in Network*: average each final feature map down to a single number and feed those numbers straight to the output.<Cite id="lin2013" /> The pooling itself has no parameters, so it cannot overfit, and the authors found it more robust to spatial shifts of the input.<Cite id="lin2013" /> ResNet ends the same way, with global average pooling and one fully connected layer.<Cite id="he2015-resnet" /> For the digits CNN, replacing flatten and both fully connected layers with global average pooling and `nn.Linear(64, 10)` leaves 19,466 parameters.

## Summary

- LeNet-5 established the CNN recipe, AlexNet scaled it with ReLU, dropout, augmentation and GPUs, and ResNet made very deep networks trainable.
- Plain deep networks suffer from degradation: worse *training* error, an optimisation problem.
- A residual block outputs $F(x) + x$; its derivative contains $+1$, so gradients always have a direct path.
- BatchNorm2d normalises each channel and needs `model.eval()` at test time; global average pooling replaces large fully connected heads.
